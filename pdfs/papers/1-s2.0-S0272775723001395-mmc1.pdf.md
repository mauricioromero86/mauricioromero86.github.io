## **B Psychometric analysis of the test**

A series of psychometric analyses were performed to ensure that the Spanish and mathematics tests used in this study were valid and reliable measures of student ability, that is, that their scores could be interpreted as reflective of student learning, and that differences in scores truly reflect differences in their learning levels.

Both item response theory (IRT) and the classical test theory were used to analyze the psychometric properties of the Spanish and mathematics tests. This is the standard procedure in the most advanced learning assessment programs with national coverage (e.g., Chile, Colombia, USA) and international coverage (e.g., PISA, TIMSS, ERCE).

For this study, the Spanish and mathematics test scores were computed using a threeparameter logistic model (3PL). All estimations were made using the multidimensional (MIRT) package in R [\(Chalmers,](#page--1-2) [2012\)](#page--1-2). Test scores were computed after removing six questions from the 2019 and 2021 tests with poor psychometric characteristics. For example, three of these questions had average response rates of less than 19 percent, while answering at random should yield a response rate of 25 percent. For the other three, the likelihood of answering the question correctly did not change with the student's ability. Final test scores were computed based on 46 items from the Spanish test in 2020/2021 and 48 from the mathematics test in 2020/2021. In 2023, all 40 questions were included. There are 15 overlapping items in each subject across 2023 and 2020/2021.

The test questions included in the final scoring had acceptable and positive discrimination values, meaning that students of higher ability were more likely to answer correctly (Figures [B.5-](#page-16-0)[B.21\)](#page-32-0). Further, the item characteristic curve fits were similar for students in both fifth and sixth grades and across assessment rounds (2020, 2021, and 2023), suggesting that comparing performance across grades and years reveals actual learning level differences.

Finally, test reliability was estimated using [\(Cronbach,](#page--1-3) [1951\)](#page--1-3)'s alpha, which measures how questions on the test are correlated. Test reliability for the 2020 and 2021 assessments was 0.84 in Spanish and 0.82 in mathematics. In 2023, reliability was 0.89 in Spanish and 0.87 in mathematics. Usually, reliability estimates of 0.7 or above are considered good in low-stake assessments [\(Nunnally,](#page--1-4) [1994;](#page--1-4) [Bland & Altman,](#page--1-5) [1997;](#page--1-5) [DeVellis,](#page--1-6) [2016;](#page--1-6) [Ram´ırez & Valverde,](#page--1-7) [2021\)](#page--1-7).
